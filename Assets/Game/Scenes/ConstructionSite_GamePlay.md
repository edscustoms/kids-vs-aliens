Kids VS Aliens — Construction Site / Level 1

Official First-Pass Gameplay Plan

Status: Official first-pass direction
Level: Construction Site / Level 1
Scope: Build the simplest complete playable version first, then improve it from real playtesting.

1. Core Goal

The Construction Site should feel like a small adventure through a real place, not a chain of disconnected objectives.

The player should:

arrive in an area that initially feels blocked and confusing,

learn how Beam Hoist works,

use the environment to progress,

find their first weapon / useful Knowledge,

reach the crash site and fight,

explore the wider yard,

discover the blocked exit,

realize the excavator may be the solution,

repair it with three components,

use it to physically open the way out,

leave the Construction Site.

The level should stay compact and readable rather than becoming a large mission-heavy map.

2. Level Size / Movement

Current Construction Site footprint is roughly:

100 m × 80 m

Player movement is now intentionally set for production gameplay:

Walk speed: 3 m/s

Sprint speed: 4.2 m/s

The previous faster movement was only for development/testing.

The level should not be made larger just to increase playtime. The goal is to make the existing space dense, readable and memorable.

3. Player Journey — First Pass

Step 1 — Arrival

Amy arrives through the automatic Beam transport in the opening area near the containers.

The player is initially restricted to a small safe pocket.

The level should make it physically clear that simply walking around the building into the later yard is not possible.

Step 2 — Find the Beam Knowledge Book

The player has little idea what to do at first.

This is one of the moments where the game can give direct guidance.

The first clear objective is:

Find / interact with the Knowledge book that teaches Beam Hoist.

The player clicks/interacts with the book and learns the Beam ability.

This is the first major progression beat.

Step 3 — Let the Player Experiment

After learning Beam, the player should have a little freedom to test it.

For example, the player may notice the electrical building or another reachable area and Beam there.

That does not have to progress the level.

This is useful because it lets the player understand:

“I can use this ability to reach elevated places.”

The game does not need to punish experimentation.

Step 4 — Container Route / First Intended Hoist

The player eventually approaches the container route.

When close enough to the intended Beam / Hoist location, the visual Hoist area appears.

This should make the solution understandable without requiring a large tutorial popup.

The player realizes:

“This is where I’m supposed to Beam.”

They use the containers and Beam Hoist to reach the building entry.

Step 5 — Short Building Route

The building should remain visually large, but the mandatory gameplay route should be short.

The player:

enters through the elevated route,

traverses a limited section of the building,

can find useful Knowledge / a weapon,

learns the basic layout,

descends once toward the main yard.

The first pass should not add extra building puzzles, switches or side missions unless later playtesting proves the section needs more gameplay.

Step 6 — Find the Gun / Additional Knowledge

During the building route, Amy obtains her first proper combat option.

This can include:

the Plasma Pistol,

another Knowledge item,

or both depending on the final placement.

The goal is to prepare the player for the first real encounter.

Step 7 — Crash Site Reveal

Amy exits / descends from the building and reaches the crash-site area.

The player should get a short moment to understand what they are seeing before combat begins.

The crash site should feel like the first major reveal of the invasion.

Step 8 — First Proper Combat Encounter

The first serious combat encounter happens around the crash site.

Initial idea:

roughly 1 vs 3

use only the existing enemy foundation

exact enemy composition and difficulty are not locked

Possible mix:

melee pressure,

pistol pressure,

rifle pressure later if it plays well.

The important part is to test this encounter in the real level and tune it from feel.

It should be challenging enough to feel satisfying, but not tuned on paper.

Step 9 — Wider Yard / Exploration

After the first fight, the player enters the more open Construction Site yard.

The player is now allowed to explore more freely.

The level should naturally lead the player toward the eventual exit area without constantly forcing objective arrows.

Step 10 — Discover the Blocked Exit

By exploring, the player eventually discovers the Construction Site exit / gate / blocked route.

The exact final obstruction can still change.

The important message is:

“This is clearly where I need to leave, but I cannot get through.”

The blockage must be physical and believable.

The player should not be able to:

walk around it,

jump over it,

Beam over it,

climb nearby props and bypass it.

Step 11 — Notice the Excavator

Near the blocked exit, the player notices the excavator.

The relationship between the machine and the blockage should be visually understandable.

Amy can say something simple, such as:

“Maybe I can use the excavator to clear this.”

This gives the player a logical next step without needing a large quest explanation.

Step 12 — Inspect the Excavator

The player approaches / interacts with the excavator.

Amy discovers that it does not work.

Simple feedback:

“It’s not working. Looks like it needs a few things.”

At this moment, the repair mini-mission activates.

4. Excavator Repair Mission

When the repair mission activates, all three required components become available at once.

The player should be able to collect them in a sensible route and return to the excavator only once.

The mission should not become:

find one item → return → find one item → return → find one item → return

Component 1 — Fuse

Gameplay purpose: observation / memory

The fuse is associated with the electrical area.

Preferred placement:

outside the electrical building,

near a maintenance bin / service area,

no need to enter or rebuild the electrical building interior.

Ideally, the player has already seen this landmark earlier.

Later, when the repair mission activates, they recognize it and return.

Component 2 — Hydraulic Fluid

Gameplay purpose: Beam recognition / traversal

The hydraulic fluid should be placed on a raised storage area / small supply dock.

The player should recognize:

“I already know how to get up there.”

The game should not replay the Beam tutorial.

This is the first important independent reuse of the ability.

Component 3 — Battery Cables

Gameplay purpose: combat

The battery cables should be placed in a small work/service area.

The player must deal with a combat encounter to reach or safely collect them.

This should be a compact fight, not another major arena.

5. Return to the Excavator

Once all three components are collected, the player returns to the excavator.

The components are delivered / applied with minimal interaction.

No elaborate repair minigame is needed.

No bespoke installation animation is required for the first pass.

The excavator becomes operational.

6. Excavator Payoff / Level Exit

The player activates the excavator.

A simple controlled action happens:

the arm / bucket moves,

the blockage is pushed, pulled, smashed or moved aside,

the exit physically becomes accessible.

The exact animation can stay simple.

The important payoff is:

Amy used a huge construction machine to physically change the level and create the escape route.

Once the route is open, the player walks out.

Construction Site / Level 1 ends here.

7. Guidance Philosophy

The level should guide the player most strongly when they genuinely have no idea what to do.

Good moments for stronger guidance:

finding the first Beam Knowledge book,

understanding the excavator repair objective,

locating active repair components if needed.

Once the player understands a mechanic, guidance should become lighter.

Desired progression:

Guide → Teach → Let Player Experiment → Player Recognizes the Solution

The game should avoid constantly placing an arrow over every single interaction.

8. Physical Progression / Anti-Shortcut Rules

Progression must be enforced primarily through believable geometry, not arbitrary invisible restrictions.

Before the building route is completed, the player must not be able to reach the later yard by accident.

Critical boundaries must be tested against:

normal walking,

sprinting,

jumping,

diagonal corner squeezing,

stepping on small props,

container roofs,

barriers,

fences,

excavator bodies / arms,

material piles,

reachable ledges,

Beam Hoist from every nearby location.

Use larger / higher / continuous physical blockers where needed.

Examples:

full-height fences / construction hoarding,

walls,

containers,

terrain,

blocked ground-floor bays,

construction materials that cannot become accidental stairs.

Do not assume a low barrier is enough.

If a player can find a weird route, the level geometry should be fixed.

9. What We Are Deliberately NOT Adding Yet

For the first pass, do not automatically add:

extra building puzzles,

extra switches,

a mandatory office mission,

a gate-key fetch quest,

multiple repair return trips,

a final extra combat wave,

more enemy archetypes,

complicated objective chains,

a large generic mission framework,

extra activities just to increase playtime.

The office, extra encounters and small side objectives can still be useful later, but they should earn their place through playtesting.

10. First-Pass Production Philosophy

The immediate goal is to build this simple version first, because it should be relatively fast to get playable.

Once the complete route works:

Play through the whole level several times.

Test it on the actual phone / production camera.

Notice where the pacing becomes boring, empty, confusing or repetitive.

Only then add something where it is actually needed.

Possible later additions could include:

an extra combat encounter,

a tiny environmental objective,

a small route-opening action,

an optional discovery,

extra enemy pressure,

a short side mission,

more environmental storytelling.

The rule is:

Do not add gameplay because the map looks empty on paper. Add gameplay because repeated playtesting proves a section needs it or is very boring.

The first version should therefore be intentionally simple, complete and fast to iterate.

Build the full playable skeleton first. Then improve the boring parts instead of guessing where they are.
